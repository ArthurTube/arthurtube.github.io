Answers:

  1. Is InternetALERT '99 working?

    As long as you have the InternetALERT icon in the system tray next to your clock, it is working.

    If you see this icon, your computer is now being protected from anyone trying to access its protected ports while you are online. To test your system, click on "Simulate Attack".

    Intruder Alert should activate, sounding an alarm. A pop-up window will display on your screen indicating that someone tried to access your computer and they were disconnected.

  2. When I try to open InternetALERT, a message tells me that my "oleaut32.dll file is out of date." What should I do?

    Please reboot your computer (shut it down and then restart it). That should take care of the problem. Please try to open InternetALERT again.

  3. When I try to open InternetALERT, I receive "msinet.ocx or one of its dependencies not correctly registered"?

    It appears that your file has gotten corrupted during the install. Or you may have an older version. You'll need the most updated version. In order to correct this problem, close all opened programs. Please rename the MSINET.OCX file in your C:\Windows\System directory  to msinet.hld and then replace it with this one: (MSINET.OCX). You may need to reboot your computer (shut it down and then restart it) for these changes to take effect.

  4. When I try to open InternetALERT, I receive " msvbvm60.dll or one of its dependencies not correctly registered"?

    It appears that your file has gotten corrupted during the install. Or you may have an older version. You'll need the most updated version. In order to correct this problem, close all opened programs. Please rename the msvbvm60.dll file in your C:\Windows\System directory  to msvbvm60.hld and then replace it with this one: (msvbvm60.dll). You may need to reboot your computer (shut it down and then restart it) for these changes to take effect.

  5. What do I do with the IP address of an intruder?

    To find out who the Intruder is click on "Find Intruder's ISP".

    If InternetALERT doesn't indicate an IP address, the intruder's ISP (Internet Service Provider) is not registered through the American Registry for Internet Numbers or the ISP is International.  Unfortunately, if they aren’t registered we won’t be able to obtain more information about the hacker. The good news is, the intruder was cut off from your computer. They won’t be able to get on to your computer to do any damage.

  6. My Attack Log shows an attack from 0.0.0.0?

    This attack is linked to some type of signal initiated by your own system, possibly another piece of software that is checking Port 80. We apologize for the inconvenience, and would like to reassure you that this is not any cause for alarm.  This address simply means that your own computer did something to set off the InternetALERT.

  7. Why are my computer’s ports open while InternetALERT is running?

In a nutshell, InternetALERT uses Microsoft's Winsock.ocx, which makes calls to the Windows wsock32.dll file to monitor the ports listed in it. It looks for any activity on those ports, and if any request to connect to one of those ports is made, it accepts the request just long enough to get relevant information from the computer making the connection. It then closes the connection to that port, without allowing any other data to get through. It also records the time, IP address, and port number of the incident. Any attempt to send data on that port will be rejected by InternetALERT.

  1. I am getting an error that says, "Input Past End of File," how can I fix this?

To fix this problem, right-click on the InternetALERT icon in your system tray in the bottom right-hand corner of your screen and choose EXIT.  Next, open My Computer > C: Drive > Program Files > Internet Alert.  In the Internet Alert folder, find your ATTACK.LOG or ATTACK file.  Just right-click on this file and delete it.  Close the open window(s).  Now open Start > Programs (or All Programs in XP) > Internet Alert and click on the Internet Alert icon.  This will restart the program and your problem should be fixed.

  1. I am trying to turn off the InternetALERT alarm sound by deleting the ALERT.WAV file, but I am still hearing an alarm.  How do I fix this?

This problem may come up if you're using Windows 2000 or Windows XP because these operating systems will substitute another alarm sound from your computer's pre-set sound scheme.  To silence the alarm when you're having this problem, right-click on the link below and choose "save target as" from the popup menu.

http://download.bonzi.com/alert.wav

When the Save As window opens, pull down the menu in the top left corner of the window and select your C: Drive.  Then, double click on the Program Files folder, then double click on the Internet Alert folder.  Click on the Save button.  If you are asked if you want to over-write the file that is there, choose YES.  After saving this file into your Internet Alert directory your alarm will be silenced.
