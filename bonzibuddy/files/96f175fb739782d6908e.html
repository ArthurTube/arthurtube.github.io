How Does It Work?

Hackers operate by "Scanning" or "Probing" for weaknesses in any one of your 65,535 ports to discover a way of breaking in. InternetALERT is an Intrusion Detection System (IDS) that works by monitoring your major and often most vulnerable ports, such as port 25 (used for e-mail) or port 443 (used for secure web browsing). If an Internet attacker attempts to "connect" to any of these monitored ports, InternetALERT instantly leaps into action and does the following:
  • sounds the alarm...
  • terminates the hacker's connection...
  • logs the attacker's information...
  • tracks down the hacker's ISP...
  • generates a report that you can easily send with one click...
  • If ISP finds that the computer's owner is the hacker, ISP cancels service. Hacker is out of business. If hacker wants to continue attacking computers, he will have to establish new service with another ISP.
  • If ISP discovers owner is innocent and computer is "zombied" (i.e. hacker has seized control for purpose of attacking other computers), ISP informs owner (who until now had absolutely no clue their system was compromised) thus allowing owner to take corrective action.

The advantages of effective detection are twofold. First, by quickly identifying unauthorized activity, you may be able to stop an attack before it happens. Second, timely detection can be used to mitigate a successful attack. If you quickly determine that a system or resource has been taken over, you can isolate and recover the system. However, if an attacker takes over your computer, and its not discovered for over a month, extensive damage can be done.

The goals of security counter-measures are to detect problems, to delay damage and to mitigate the effects of an attack. Intrusion detection systems can recognize the first hallmarks of attack, mitigating damage. In addition, when other security devices fail, due to configuration, attack, or user error, intrusion detection systems can recognize the problem and notify the right people. For example, it is far better for you to determine that your firewall is incorrectly configured immediately than to discover this after an attacker has successfully penetrated it.

Prevention, Detection, and Response

Security can be broken down into three categories:

  • Prevention is similar to placing deadbolt locks on your doors, locking your windows, and perhaps installing a fence around your yard.

  • Detection is the installation of burglar alarms, security cameras, and motion detectors.

  • Response would be wiring your burglar alarm to the police department or a private security company.

Within the world of information security, prevention tools include firewalls, authentication, strong passwords, digital certificates, encryption, etc. Detection tools include Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS), anti-virus, anti-Trojan, etc. Thus, InternetALERT should not be used in isolation as your only form of security, but should be deployed alongside other security tools to provide "security in depth." For example, InternetALERT should be run in conjunction with a firewall and anti-virus program in order to achieve maximum security.
