About Voice E-Mail's Lossless Audio Compression
   

Before sound compression, it was unfeasible to communicate online using digital audio because "digital waveform data" creates such large files, that the time and cost to transfer them was too high. Voice E-Mail 4.0 uses the latest in digital audio compression technology to compress messages before transferring them through CompuServe, America Online, Microsoft Network, and the Internet.


Voice E-Mail 4.0's audio compression is "lossless" -- which means it doesn't lose any "digital waveform data" during compression. Most of the audio compression algorithms you encounter today are "lossy" -- which means they lose digital waveform data during compression (usually by purposely throwing parts of the data out). The problem is that the more data is lost, the more the quality degrades. And there's no way to get the data back once it's gone, which means recordings such as music will never again sound exactly like the original.

"Lossless" compression, on the other hand, does not lose ANY of the original data. This is why "lossless" compression algorithms are much more difficult to build. Have you ever noticed that "lossy" recordings have a somewhat "hissy" and "warbly" sound to them? That's because you are not listening to the original recording. "Lossless" recordings, on the other hand, sound EXACTLY like the original. Nothing is thrown away; nothing is lost. So it makes for a superior solution.
