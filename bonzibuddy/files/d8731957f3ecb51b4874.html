Recording/Playing

  1. How can people who don't have the Voice E-Mail program receive and hear my Voice E-Mail messages?

    Voice E-Mail 3.0 comes with two major programs: one for creating, compressing, and sending Voice E-Mail messages, the other for receiving, uncompressing, and playing them. It is this second program, the Voice E-Mail 3.0 Player, that has been packaged and made freely distributable so that Voice E-Mail recipients do not have to have the software in order to uncompress and play them. The free player may be downloaded at any time from http://www.bonzi.com/freeplay/vemplay.htm.

  2. When I play back my recordings, I hear silence (no sound at all), static, or "distant" audio (i.e. very low volume). What should I do?

    First, know that your recordings should be loud and clear, and that if they are not, this means your audio settings need to be adjusted on your computer. The most common solutions for this are listed below. Note: You may want to download this sample Voice E-Mail to hear the quality you should expect to get on your own recordings.

    1) First, before you do anything else, make sure your microphone is plugged into your Sound Card and NOT your Modem. This is a very common problem because those two devices look so similar when you look at the back of your computer, and many modems have microphone jacks just like your Sound Card. So check this first.

    2) If that doesn't fix it:

    1. Click the "Start" button.
    2. Click on Programs-Accessories-Multimedia-Volume Control.
    3. Click on the "Options" menu and select "Properties."
    4. Select the "Recording" option (just below the "Playback" option). It should change the information in the bottom part of the window.
    5. Make sure the "Recording Control" checkbox and the "Microphone" checkbox are checked.
    6. Click OK. The Recording Control screen will display a volume slider for each control. The volume sliders should each be about 75% of the way to the top.
    7. At the bottom of each volume slider, there are check boxes that toggle between "Select" and "Mute." Make sure that they are selected (checked) instead of muted (unchecked).

    3) If that doesn't fix it, run this basic Sound Card test to see if your computer is even setup to record and play audio:

    1. Click the "Start" button.
    2. Click on Programs-Accessories-Multimedia-Sound Recorder. (If you are running Windows 3.x, click on the "Sound Recorder" in your "Accessories" group).
    3. In the "Sound Recorder" program, click the "Edit" menu and then click "Audio Properties" (Win95 only).
    4. Make sure it is set to "Telephone Quality" (which is 8-bit 11khz) and click OK.
    5. In the "Sound Recorder" program, now click on the "Record" button, hold your microphone up close to your mouth, and count to 5 or 10.
    6. Click the "Stop" button and then click the "Play" button.

    If you hear silence (no sound at all), static, or "distant" audio (i.e. the volume is really low), this means your computer is not currently setup to record and play audio. Contact your computer or Sound Card manufacturer to find out if there is something you need to do -- any special requirements -- to be able to record with your Microsoft Sound Recorder. Because once we know that your computer can record and play a simple audio clip in Microsoft Sound Recorder, Voice E-Mail should work flawlessly.

  3. Can I record for longer than sixty seconds?

    To record longer messages, simply press the record button again. This will extend your recording time in 60 second increments

  4. I have received a Voice E-Mail message, but I don’t know how to play it.

    When you receive a Voice E-Mail (.VEM) file, simply double click the "Attachment" link or icon. A double click is all it takes because Voice E-Mail automatically detects if it's a Voice E-Mail, and if it is, runs the Voice E-Mail Player, un-compresses the .VEM file for you, and then loads both the digital audio and the picture. The Voice E-Mail Auto-Load Feature works with most Internet e-mail applications -- such as Eudora, Microsoft Exchange, etc.

    Note: You may have to use this Save As feature for the first Voice E-Mail message you receive in order to associate the Voice E-Mail Player with the .VEM file extension.

    The Save As feature: If you prefer to save the file to your hard drive, just click once on the attachment with the right mouse button and select Save As from the menu that pops up. Select the folder to which you would like to save the message. This will be the same folder you will need to access from the OPEN button in the Voice E-Mail player. To play the message, bring up the Voice E-Mail player, then click on Open. Select the folder to which you saved the .VEM file, then select the message you want to play back and click OK. The player will uncompress the file and load it for playback.

    If you are an AOL user, you will need to download the .vem file, then open it with the player.

    To manually download Voice E-Mail (.VEM) files:

    1. Click the "Download File" button.
    2. Click the "OK" button to download into your America Online "download" directory.
    3. The file will download to your computer.

    To automatically download Voice E-Mail (.VEM) files:

    1. From the "Mail" menu, select "FlashSessions."
    2. Make sure the boxes "Retrieve unread mail" and "attached files" are checked.
    3. Click "Activate Session Now." (Note: You can activate a flash session online or offline).
    4. All regular e-mail and Voice E-Mail files will be downloaded to your computer. You'll then see them listed in your "Incoming FlashMail" box.

    After the message is downloaded, simply select the Voice E-Mail Player and click on Open. Select your AOL Download folder, then select the message you would like to play back and click 'OK.' The player will uncompress the file and load it for play back.

  5. When I click on the record button a message comes up that says, "vemaudio.wav is in use by another application (?) and you can either interrupt it or wait until it is done."

Reboot the computer. Connect up to your service provider and run this basic Sound Card test to see if your computer is even setup to record and play audio:

1. Click the "Start" button.

2. Click on Programs-Accessories-Multimedia-Sound Recorder. (If you are running Windows 3.x, click on the "Sound Recorder" in your "Accessories" group).

3. In the "Sound Recorder" program, click the "Edit" menu and then click "Audio Properties" (Win95 only).

4. Make sure it is set to "Telephone Quality" (which is 8-bit 11khz) and click OK.

5. In the "Sound Recorder" program, now click on the "Record" button, hold your microphone up close to your mouth, and count to 5 or 10.

6. Click the "Stop" button and then click the "Play" button.

If you can’t not record while online, then that means that your problem is not with the Voice E-Mail but with your Sound card and MS sound recorder. You need to call your sound card vendor and have them help you get the MS sound recorder working first at 8-bit-11khz (telephone Quality), then we can move on to Voice E-Mail recording!
